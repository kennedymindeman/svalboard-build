# Keyboards and hardware

Message ids in parentheses are the last 6 digits of Discord snowflakes. Bare ids resolve in `discord/raw/channel-389438169520799746.jsonl` (#general); ids prefixed `H:` resolve in `discord/raw/channel-530711772496396288.jsonl` (#hardware). Suffixes collide across and within the exports, so keep the prefix and disambiguate by date. Agreement counts are floor estimates.

## Board size and form factor

- **A 60% is the practical floor, and it works.** No F-row key is used; the F-row reaches menus only and can go on a layer (353006, 877598, 300865, 128474, 139658, 930445, 221936, 116810, 220393); Edennil ran one unmodified ~5 years (358495, 777104, 948999). Use the file matching your layout (424818). No right-side Alt is fatal (596230); some need right Ctrl swapped with menu and its cap pulled (165234). Mell00yell00's rule (2023-01): "any 60% board and larger will work with the core… an F-row is not necessary but some people utilize it" (H:140216). Edennil recommends 60% for the same reasons people adopt TheCore (H:574848): "A lot of similar reasons that some people move to The Core are present in 60% keyboards. / You never have to move your hands to accomplish everything you do in a normal keyboard. / You don't lose functionality, the functionality is instead built into layers on the keyboard." Plainly: "I strongly recommend 60% keyboards" (H:166750).
- **65% needs a software remap**: left arrow to Alt, Shift and Ctrl swapped (597388, 790484, 139059); on a Ducky, left arrow, Ctrl and Shift, Fn cap pulled (709027, 437491). doink's 65% board question drew no reply (H:036480).
- **75%: pop the left-arrow cap for a modifier** — Ctrl, plus a second Alt on down-arrow for the co-op top bar (757274, 189642, 180787), or Shift (586320). Poletes: "75 % is like the optimal layout imo" (H:560448).
- **TKL was the 2020-05 house preference**, on desk space (988339, 685810). Half-size laptop arrows are unusable (714507, 750528).
- **The 1800/96% split the room** (889922): unworkable, better a $50 dedicated board (131551, 047091); or pull the left-arrow cap, or make it a third modifier under the ring finger on right Alt (187689, 339713); ScaryMouse finds it near his own layout, fine with Ctrl on MO(1) (456286).
- **40% is hard but doable** with a macropad thumb cluster (414528, 749542); the Keyboardio Atreus defeated everyone, wanting three modifiers plus a reachable function key (813492). TheCore uses ~32 keys and few per layer, so a 28-key gamo-28 fits with layering (514098).
- **Avoid the HHKB**, worst layout for this (299389). The Microsoft Natural Ergonomic 4000 curves the wrong way; backspace would need ~40 degrees of tilt (853155, 416514). Razer: entry-level quality, high price (477156). The Magicforce Smart 68 does not work — JaKaTaK, "Nope" (H:265792), "You can't easily press alt and shift at the same time with the thumb" (H:343582), "Which is read by the game as ctrl+shift in plus" (H:850132), against Altafen's earlier guess that it would work (H:358467); the general rule behind the verdict is "you just need a key to be there, whether it's menu or alt, as long as it's programmable" (H:069888).
- **Edennil's two buying rules, 2022-02.** First, "make sure that the keyboard doesn't try to save space by making the arrow keys take up the right control slot... and then squeeze everything that is normally between spacebar and right control into the smaller space" (H:820117). Second, Core+ only: "If the keyboard has an Fn/Pn key directly to the left of right-control, you'll want to read the manual to see if the location is movable" (H:450665). Burwicke's version: "I want a keyboard without two buttons between the right side ctrl+alt (ie windows and fn or menu key), one's fine but two is uncomfortable" (H:020904); a Ducky One 2 SF passed that test (H:446219). Edennil keeps these answers canned in a starcraft-faq channel and pastes them here (H:121182, H:577729).
- **What is right of the spacebar decides it.** Mell00yell00 (H:037963): "the most important thing to consider is what is going on to the right of the spacebar because these are where your modifiers are and we cant move that FN key with authotkeys"; "It is probably best to find a board which doesnt have a fn key at all" (H:737236). Boards with no Fn: the Corsair K70 "doesnt have a fn key at all so you have 4 buttons to the right of the spacebar that are remappable" (H:228306); the Das Keyboard 4 has no Fn but separate media keys (H:793794). The Cooler Master mk730's macro key is not remappable (H:228306), and the Leopold FC980M is good quality but "cannot be programmed" (H:020996). Market summary, 2020: nothing works "unless it comes with programable software or you can also get a filco board and the pegasus hoof chip" (H:008704).
- **Buy hotswap for a first board** so you can change your mind about switches (H:548490); TOMES dissents, "Hotswap PCB is an awesome feature but not the most practical for gamers" (H:696010). Retrofitting millmax sockets needs desoldering anyway (H:909032).
- **Budget picks over time**: a $45 hotswappable aliexpress 60% (H:057606); the GK61/GK707 at $60 — "The gk707 is the tenkeyless version that I have ($5 cheaper)... the least expensive keyboard you can find thats reliable that also has a hotswappable pcb. This is truly an excellent 'budget buy'" (H:011861); the Redragon k587/k587-pto at $60 (H:765573). By 2020-11 the Hexgears GK707 is "the cheapest hotswap u can find on the market" at $50 shipped (H:487370) and Mell00yell00's own SC2 board, used "for 4 plus years" (H:576991): "the fn key is in the right location to be removed for the core and the left arrow key can always be modified via autohotkey" (H:129094). The Tecware Phantom is "basically the same thing" (H:128735). Above that: **Ducky at $90-120 for the bottom-row dipswitches**, "dipswitches on the back allowing you to reconfigure your bottom row of modifiers... not hot swappable", so switch changes mean desoldering (H:133693, H:487370); **GMMK TKL at ~$100-110 hotswap**, "nothing amazing but gets the job done" (H:686450), which Poletes prefers "purely for the ability to use qmk... or i guess via" (H:439198). The Drop ALT is "a solid keyboard, but you are sure paying for it" with cheaper Ducky options (H:314418). Generally: "cannot stress enough how nice a 'proper' mechanical keyboard feels compared to Logitech or Razer" (H:651904).
- **ISO enter is fine; `\` just lands better** (981492, 357760). Mell00yell00 argues ISO is actively good — "ISO enter ftw... You got a flexible bottoms row with your modifiers and can pull your winkey to access shift" (H:987388) — while playing ANSI himself (H:797671); Bose found ISO's shift too far away (H:466665).
- **No SC2 board is sold**; buy any programmable mechanical with 6-7 columns per hand (586782); no purpose-built Core keypad exists (566054); "there is no keyboard designed for sc2" holds through 2022 (H:324550), and the standing answer is "any QMK keyboard basically... and make layer for games" (H:782825). The one exception arrives in 2024 and is still closed beta: Posey's board (H:268136), below. Mell00yell00's 2021 ideal off-the-shelf board: standard bottom row, four 1.25u modifiers right of a 6.25u spacebar, the 1.25u cap under `/` removed, 1.75u shift with ISO enter for an extra index-reachable key (959046, 050089, 088286), split right shift (262949).
- **Nothing you buy makes you better**: "there is not an option that will make u better or worse at sc2" (H:978621, H:533952).
- Asked and never answered: fenr1r's request for a list of boards with a reprogrammable Fn, where the only name anyone had was the discontinued KÛL ES-87 (H:718756); Siaal's NK65 v2 shopping (H:596531); BristleBumpkin's curved-keyboard and thumb-rest question (H:143061); TobyTheKiwi's search for an affordable TKL with a volume scroll, since the Corsair K63 has buttons not a scroll (H:267650, H:040405). Generic keyboard questions get referred out to [r/MechanicalKeyboards' wiki](https://www.reddit.com/r/MechanicalKeyboards/wiki/index) (H:159114).
- In passing: Pok3r and Redox (460065, 968916), Roccat Vulcan/Titan (360773), Roccat Isku FX (829150), Steelseries Merc (399446), Leopold FC750R PD with a hardware Ctrl/Capslock toggle (798868), Kira 96% (890816), Niz Plum Micro 82 (615309), Keychron C3 Pro (398782). Edennil's own rig, verbatim (H:158304): "Split keyboard is a Redox with 65g Zelios Switches. This is what I currently use. / 60% is a Pok3r with Cherry Brown Switches, Green Tea Keycaps, and Wood Case."

## Key budgets: per finger and per key

- **Judge a board by per-finger key budget** (495781): 3-4 thumb (three modifiers plus `/`), 8-9 pinky (`U` floats), 4-5 ring, 4-5 middle, 9-11 index (`0` and `/` float); splits clear it easily (073694). Principles: modifiers on the thumb, no repeated finger in a sequence, high-priority actions on high-priority keys (287604).
- **Sizing against a device instead of a hand**: Edennil measures the layout as "7-8 keys horizontally and 4 keys vertically" (H:998283, H:665311).
- **Three modifiers, never at once.** "you don't press all 3 at the same time" (H:138528); "the 3rd modifier is pressed less often the the other 2 / but you do press all 3" (H:419585). The stronger 2024 form, which sets the per-key budget on a custom board: "If a key is assigned 3 modifiers, it means that you are not intended to press it" (H:503252, H:432340).
- **On a firmware gaming layer the budget is per key, not per finger — eight combinations each.** Edennil, verbatim (H:818162): "If they are in a line, what I can do with the letter A: / - No modifier + A / - Control + A / - Shift + A / - Alt + A / - Fn + A / - Control + Shift + A / - Alt + Control + A / - Fn + Alt + A" — "That's 8 possible options on every single key if I need" (H:269688). Constraints: "At most, I only press 2 modifiers at a time" (H:069756); a pair has to be physically adjacent, "I would press my thumb between the keys labeled `Bs` and `Delete` to press both of those keys at the same time. In the gaming layer that would press `Ctrl` and `Shift` together" (H:827900); where they are not adjacent, "use one key as a momentary layer key (this is what the Fn key on a normal keyboard does)" (H:093714).

## The Fn key is the one thing to check before buying

- **Fn is the only key software cannot remap — it never reaches the OS** (102441, 584576; H:405706, "afik the os never gets it"). Gooey's long form (H:205416): "On many standard keyboards FN will not be remappable as the key by itself does not send any signal. Programmable keyboards can often remap FN via keyboard specific software/firmware." Poletes: "Can't do fn unless you have keyboard firmware" (H:935563). Mell00yell00 held the strongest form — "you cannot make a FN a ctl alt or shift modifier using autohotkey. Its the only key on the board you cant change" (H:966549) — and conceded once Edennil showed the firmware route (H:969345). SharpKeys cannot see it (546826), which sank a Leopold 660m plan (193860). Check where Fn sits (816498) and that it moves (197226); a HK GAMING GK61 couldn't, so DeathDealer switched to an 80% EP-84 Epomaker (726397, 722951).
- **The 2021-11 statement of the rule** (Mell00yell00): "what your looking for in a board that doesnt have dipswitches is the location of the FN key" / "that is the only key that cannot be moved or reprogrammed" / "typically this cap is removed so your thumb can more easily reach the / and shift keys" (H:318612, H:055740, H:097906).
- **Ducky moves Fn with underside dipswitches**, on the One and One 2 (076184, 555978, 159573, 889898, 916796); Rego's rear dipswitches give him "alt fn apps ctrl" (H:234240). Edennil's One 2 Mini table: DIP1/DIP2 both OFF gives the default alt, windows, fn, ctrl, the other three combinations rotating those (558424). On a Ducky Mecha Mini, put Alt on Fn (198826). **The 60% Ducky is the exception Mell00yell00 warns about** (H:107461): "be careful if you plan on using the core with the 60% ducky. you cant move the location of the FN key to put a modifier there... it sits right under the 'shift' key next to RControl. The reason i sold my ducky one tkl." Pulling that cap leaves a gap between ctl and the new alt (H:866179) and makes `/` hard to thumb (H:622804).
- **Pok3r**: dipswitches move Fn; Fn and Pn remap to anything but right control, so swap them (525757, 860097). Edennil uses **Pn** as his third modifier and "I moved the location of fn and pn" (H:080778). The general point (H:743874, H:145994): "Ya once you move Fn, it doesn't matter what the other keys are / Because you can use Fn to reprogram every single key to be whatever you want", i.e. "Fn allows you to reprogram all the other keys except for itself. / The dip switch allows you to move Fn." But **Fn alone does not send Alt** — fine for Alt+key jump-to-location (394334), finicky for camera *centering* (074068, 962940).
- **No dipswitch**: pull the Fn cap (849660), or look for Fn remapping or QMK (973002, 388015); the Aula F75 remaps Fn to right Alt (806738). Lenovo laptops swap ctrl and fn in BIOS (H:515266). On JohnSolo's WASD Hotswap V3 the menu key *is* Fn (H:263902), and "Many have a dipswitch or other mechanism for moving the Fn key" (H:759825); the advice there was "I'd move the Fn key to be next to Right Alt. And make the menu/list key = alt" (H:580914). The standing 2020-06 answer for any board: it depends, check the manual on the manufacturer's product page, "It's usually documented" (H:010113, H:001020).
- **Superseded but instructive: rather than replacing Fn, macro Fn+key to unused F-keys.** Edennil, 2020-01: "I actually strongly PREFER the Fn key in place of one of the modifiers" (H:604551); "If you have Fn right next to control, I think that's a bonus" (H:838401); "Instead changing Fn to be Alt or some other modifier, just remap Fn + key to be some Random F key" (H:681922) — "And had like Fn + P go to F13, Fn + O go to F14, etc" (H:256033), "It allowed me to do things like bind Fn + J to inject. / And then just hold the modifier the whole sequence" (H:310347). SC2 only sees the F-key, so it "registers it as you only sending a single key" (H:877251) with the "Exact same number of key presses" (H:170752); "(pre 6.0, this was what I did to get around the inject problem)" (H:315136). It works because SC2 cannot bind Modifier+Key as an ability on the command card (H:302135) but does recognise F13 and F14 (H:157574, H:130369). Done on a Ducky One 2 and a Pok3r by Edennil and tso (H:841602, H:639538), not through SharpKeys or AHK but "using the programming mode of your keyboard" (H:138762) — "The Ducky One uses Ducky Macro v2.0" (H:074956). **Superseded, per Edennil: "With version 6 this shouldn't be necessary" (H:353566), "at this point in the core, it isn't as critical" (H:844299).**
- **So a board whose Fn sits in a modifier position is not unusable** — the standing conclusion for shoppers (H:886110, H:849731): "you just need to make some modifications to the hotkey file and your keyboard."
- **Beware vendor gaming modes and lighting keys.** A Logitech MX Keys puts an unbindable Fn where Application/Menu was, and its owner gave up on the board; a Razer TKL appears to rewrite Apostrophe to Grave in the file with Gaming Mode on (042241, 652756). Arrogfire's Alt was a lighting key Windows never sees; fix was moving Alt anywhere between space and control (383750).

## Firmware remapping beats software remapping

- **Put the modifier remap in firmware — QMK or VIA.** It lives in the keyboard, survives Windows updates, needs no background process and no cloud, travels to any PC, and matches tournament rules, where pros cannot install software (190652); a Keychron C3 Pro was reconfigured 10 minutes after delivery (398782). Same case in 2020: a dedicated SC2 layer survives alt-tabbing and cannot be flagged (720031, 890851, 514629, 136384). Poletes' best way is a QMK profile or sublayer, Alt on an arrow key, Shift/Ctrl swapped (052610, 733837); firmware beats AutoHotkey for anything permanent (044551). QMK layers also give TheCore in Brood War without AutoHotkey (420168).
- **If you are buying new, buy a QMK or VIA board.** Bitstorm, 2020: "if I was in the market for a new keyboard I think I'd be looking at QMK support" (H:872074); Edennil: "QMK is amazing firmware" (H:455681); Wolta Horo's Razer replacement is "a QMK board so it's fully reprogrammable" (H:465822). Mell00yell00 on VIA: "if the board isnt oem it prob uses qmk or via which will allow you to reprogram easily" (H:314505); VIA "almost certainly remaps FN key" — "yes" (H:988374); "ya via is very easy to use" (H:099261). Entry points are [qmk.fm and config.qmk.fm](https://qmk.fm) (H:681876, H:235670); MatosMachine flashes his ErgoDox EZ from Linux without trouble (H:732573).
- **Every split ergo board is firmware-customisable, and the gaming layer is what you build with it.** Edennil, 2024-02 (H:669781): "For anyone that uses a split Ergo keyboards... they **all** will be customizable via keyboard firmware. The 2 most common are QMK and VIA. Using either of these, you will have access to ~10 different layers on your keyboard. Those of us that game on these keyboards will press a button on them to go into the 'gaming layer'. On the gaming layer, the key difference is that all of the modifier keys are put on the thumb cluster (similar to what you would do if you used the Core+)." And: "It's a keyboard firmware thing, not a specific keyboard thing" (H:667200). His own layer (H:380800): "This is what I do for my gaming layer on my Redox keyboard. The thumb cluster presses Control, Shift, Alt, Fn, and Space. This lets me layer hotkeys a lot more. I can assign any combination of `control, alt, shift, Fn, and/or no modifier` + `any key` to a hotkey in starcraft" — [image](https://cdn.discordapp.com/attachments/389438169520799746/1138916700406960148/Hotkeys.png) (H:299698); those keys are "**only** like that when I am in the gaming layer" (H:579452). ZMK behaves the same way and "Using ZMK doesn't impact rapid-fire at all" (H:196486). The same machinery gives you ordinary layers too: "For example I'd make a layer where I had rebound 890IOPKL; to be a numpad" (H:581834).
- **Bitstorm's Filco controller swap is the fullest hardware-only solution anyone built.** He replaced the Filco Majestouch 2 controller with a Pegasus Hoof running EasyAVR, giving (H:447306) "`Qwerty UK => Colemak UK => Colemak UK with CorePlus alt/shift/ctrl` rotation pushing all that into the hardware, so a hardware compatible CorePlus keyboard." [Write-up with photos](https://ghost.lolcathost.co.uk/filco-majestouch-controller-mod-colemak-starcraft/) (H:235604). [EasyAVR](https://github.com/dhowland/EasyAVR) "essentially allows you to map anything to anything along with a bunch of macro support and has 10 independent layers" (H:154066); `Alt-Gr + F2` flips to Core, `Alt-Gr + F12` swaps qwerty/colemak, and "the solution is entirely contained within the keyboard so it's a plug and play solution." His two hardware-only options (H:177492): buy something firmware-programmable, or mod what you have — Filco plus Pegasus Hoof, or a Pok3r out of the box (H:675139). Motivation: on Linux with Colemak he had to build a Colemak UK variant himself, X11 only, consoles reverting to qwerty; firmware made it OS-agnostic (H:033280). He then found the board already in `qmk_firmware` under `keyboards/bpiphany/pegasushoof` (H:588864) and concluded "I think by swapping out the Filco controller I essentially added QMK support" (H:070337), though he runs EasyAVR and its `.json` isn't compatible (H:541415). The stock QMK Pegasus Hoof layout has right control switching layer 0 to layer 1 (multimedia) and the right app key resetting to layer 0 (H:957185), with an open question he never answered: "it is not clear to me whether you press right control to switch the layer, or hold it down, I think the difference is important" (H:154112). He also opened an ISO-support pull request on the QMK Configurator (H:324746). Which boards take custom firmware at all: [deskthority's list](https://deskthority.net) (H:706579).
- **The LAN argument is the oldest form**: prefer firmware if you play on other machines or at LANs (798868, 871936) — also why Edennil recommended the **Ducky One 2** in 2019-09, its firmware setting repeat delay and rate faster than Windows allows (477156). **Partly contradicted later**: Mell00yell00 sold his One 2 for lacking key remapping (057967), and Bitstorm was unsure whether the feature was firmware or driver (809421); his answer was a Filco Majestouch modded for firmware rewrites, plug and play (586183, 084928).
- **Before QMK, use the board's own internal remapping.** bunfoof's Keychron K2 fix: "I have a keyboard with a similar layout and used its internal functionality to swap the ctrl and left arrow buttons on a separate layer than my normal typing layer" (H:569105), endorsed by Mell00yell00 (H:504138); you reach that firmware through a dipswitch or a key combination (H:160627).
- **Not programmable? The 1upkeyboards USB-to-USB converter through TMK** (407452). QMK covers what the game won't but is not very accessible (101143).
- **Software routes, in order**: registry (SharpKeys), PowerToys (535167), vendor software, AutoHotkey last. Disabling an AHK script needs a log off and back on (410044), and it must be scoped with `#ifWinActive StarCraft II` so the remap does not follow you out of the game (213623, 659532). **SharpKeys gotcha**: right Ctrl mapped to Alt sends Ctrl+Alt, because it lands on AltGr; map it to Left shift instead (686451, diagnosed 260487).
- **AutoHotkey is only ever used for 1-to-1 remaps**: "We us [sic] AHK which also has the capability of programming sequences, but we only use it for 1 to 1" (H:236865). The canonical snippet, from an AZERTY board with no right Ctrl (H:791860):

  ```
  #IfWinActive StarCraft II
  Up::LShift
  Right::LControl
  Down::LAlt
  #IfWinActive
  ```

  with the Core+ variant `Up::LControl` / `Right::LAlt` / `Down::LShift`; save it as `.ahk` and run it (H:375446). That user went with PowerToys instead (H:027143). SharpKeys also fixes caps lock tabbing SC2 out (H:356478), and a premade AHK ctrl/shift swap script sits in the project Drive — JaKaTaK, "pretty sure it's in the drive with everything else" (H:297299).
- **Whether AHK is reliable inside SC2 is genuinely unsettled.** Edennil's workaround is to split the two (H:171604, H:494002, H:919519): "several people have had issues with AHK in sc2"; "the common workaround is use sharpkeys to change your keys"; "and then use AHK to change them to normal outside of sc2". Dissent: Mell00yell00, "i have yet to have any issues with ahk in sc2 and its pretty easy to use" (H:739924); Siaal, "i literally just RControl::LAlt and...it seems like it works fine" (H:496202), with one residual bug — binding control+alt by hitting shift+control registers only the control, while control+shift works (H:027264) — and he finds AHK "actually more flexible than sharpkeys since you can set up combinations" (H:161365). The 2019-02 failure case: `#MenuMaskKey RAlt` and `#MenuMaskKey LAlt` did not fix it (H:202176, H:913664); SelfAtari, "Eventually you get to the problem that Ctrl+Shift registers as all three modifiers" (H:347393). He bought a Razer Blackwidow to get around it (H:642251) and later confirmed SharpKeys plus AHK in the startup folder "worked well enough, just a bit annoying" (H:912833), with the caveat it may break other games (H:682954). Edennil's diagnosis of the general case: it shows up when people bind to right alt, which was AltGr (H:041172). Fyfelka's fallback is to rewire in the registry then use AHK to restore normal use (H:442890), summarised "for now, there is no good way to setup plus version, if AHK fails on you" (H:425800).
- **SharpKeys' documented limits**, quoted from its GitHub readme by Kleppa (H:288168): "Things that SharpKeys will not do: / Map multiple key presses to one key - e.g. it will not support an attempt to remap Ctrl+C to the F5 key / Map mouse clicks to any key / Support certain hardware keys that never make it to Windows - e.g. Logitech's volume buttons or most Fn keys / Support multiple mappings for different users - the Windows key being tweaked is for an entire machine..."
- Vendor software that worked: the Niz Plum Micro 82 tool (615309), Razer Synapse on a Huntsman (039809, 770089), SteelSeries Apex Pro for macros and modifier rebinds (345695); G Hub and Synapse also named (260989, 717810). Infographics were drawn with keyboard-layout-editor.com (362500). Unanswered: PowerToys modifiers not sticking (H:741835) and a bare Logitech G Hub question (H:150324).

## Moving the modifiers for TheCore+

- **Goal: all three modifiers adjacent and thumb-reachable with the hand at home** (005446, 601115, 147521). Standing 2024 advice: **move Alt onto the Menu/List key left of right-Control**, no stated disadvantage (541284, 480852), matching 2020's "modifiers in the control/shift/list positions" (839508, 216593). Keys worth stealing: app/menu, right Ctrl, right Alt (482916, 307964).
- **Remap at the key-location level, not in SC2.** Rebinding "Shift+key" to "Menu+key" in-game loses SC2's hard-coded shift behaviour; a firmware/AHK/SharpKeys remap does not, since the game still sees Shift (966224, 186828). Re-check the modifier combinations in the spreadsheet after moving anything (388264).
- **It is optional.** 6g does not assume you can remap modifiers (067085, 852746); the remap is ideal but mostly ergonomic (103207). Keys between Control and Alt are fine too, since Alt only sets base cameras (526678, 283462). Siaal's version: Core Plus used the rotation for extra control-group and camera modifiers, but "the core 6 doesn't really ahve any way to abuse that though so it's mostly there for people who like the modifiers in the new places" (H:448202, H:285835), and "you don't *need* to rotate them to play normally" (H:917642). Arkonite runs only menu-to-alt (H:070592) and Siaal does much the same (H:453964).
- **Under Core+ the live keys are right-Ctrl, appskey and wherever Alt moved; physical Alt is vestigial** (128370, 577172, 607116). So **do not pull the right shift and right windows caps**: maxisq did, following an old video, and could not deselect units or queue commands, because Core+ shift lives on appskey — the cap he had pulled (063134, 547853, 866567, 480187).
- **Which of Ctrl and Shift goes under the thumb is openly unsettled** (H:388073). jaydik's tiebreaker: "Shift key has the largest footprint on the keyboard so put the most used function on that" (H:031382).
- **marin's 2020-10 bottom-row experiment is the cautionary tale.** He reordered his bottom-right to menu -> ctrl -> alt -> fn (H:451965), then found "its hard to press ctrl+shift at the same time" because "alt is directly below shift and ctrl is a bit to the side" (H:973097), worse with small hands (H:906762). Menu-as-cancel misfires — "multiple times ive tried to hit ctrl but cancelled instead" (H:217460) — and his fix was tactile, not positional; see the artisan cap below.
- Worked mappings: Mell00yell00 — Shift = Shift, List = Ctrl, Ctrl = Alt, Alt = `z`, his trash key (721696); jaydik — Fn pulled, list to shift, control to alt, shift to control (959658); Poletes — Left Arrow as Alt, the other arrows unbound after scrolling the screen by accident (459647, 891378).
- **On a compact board, line Ctrl and Shift up and put Alt as close as possible.** JaKaTaK for a Leopold 660m, whose bottom row breaks the Ctrl+Shift camera modifier (706624): shift = ctrl, fn = shift, left of fn = alt (599488, 644057, 587064).
- **Edennil's 5.0 Pok3r setup**: Fn rather than Alt as the modifier, three physical positions kept, every "Alt + key" bind rewritten "Fn + key", plus a layer mapping Fn+key to an arbitrary key (670899, 393812).

- **NoTruePunk's SharpKeys remap, 2022**, posted in full: right side becomes Ctrl, Shift, menu -> left Alt, Win -> right Alt/AltGr, exploiting SC2 reading AltGr as Ctrl+Alt (198484, 526859, permalink 803247309977944065); he then merged cameras onto the control-group keys (camera = Alt+key, set camera = Ctrl+Alt+key or AltGr+key), freeing `G` and `B` (411081, 430250, 493461). ## Split, ergonomic and columnar boards

- **Split ortholinear is the natural home for the layout.** TheCore moves your hand to the wrong side of the board only to get modifiers under the thumb, which a split does natively — why the translation was easy for Edennil (369492, 921680); a split's left half looks like the right side of a normal board at the same spacing (097745). Too few keys on one side means QMK tricks (647987). Adapting to an ortholinear board is "Easy." / "Especially with the no tilt recommendation right now" (H:369985, H:553536); his own board is split and ortholinear (H:711124) and he runs "my own version. / i had to cusomize it for redox" (H:755146).

For gaming, it kind of depends... If you already use TheCore+, I don't think it's a huge improvement. The Core+ already puts 3 modifiers directly under your thumb... ## Keyboard rotation

- **Rotation is no longer required.** It was expected in v5, where `/` was thumb-only; v6 allows thumb or index (553092, 765697, 456902). Several report little or no tilt (895242, 837669, 026867, 573310), though JuicyJuuce still rotates his (533994). **v6 was designed for a flat board**, index finger lowered instead (681802, 370004, 970857, 647114) — good for laptop users (479116).
- **"Tilt" means rotating the board off parallel with the desk edge, not raising the feet** (842618).
- **Why it helps when it does**: rotating clockwise puts `8` directly above `I`, the ortholinear idea, and moves the modifiers where the thumb can press two at once without wrist strain (344906 with photo; 434792, 434517); go until the columns `8,i,k,,` and `9,[o,l,.]` align naturally (766390). That is also Edennil's canned answer, "Similar idea to ortholinear keyboards" (H:032151), and the standing reply when a thumb can't reach Shift: "maybe 15-30 degrees... I like to rotate it until the columns 8,i,k,, and 9,o,l,. are vertical" (H:108837). Poletes' first fix for an uncomfortable modifier reach (817193, 868328).
- **Angles over time.** v1-5 wanted 30-45 clockwise (459951, 697237), with Edennil's photo (708841, 456744, 920286). 2020-09: jaydik 10-15 (269665), Poletes ~30 (642316), Mell00yell00 none but set back far enough to rest his forearm (748426). 2021: Mell00yell00 ~10, telling a newcomer at 40 to back off (979520). 2022-23 cluster low — 10-15 (254952, 306344), ~10 (372830), 5-10 (292392), 20 (524998) — with 30 still the standing answer for reach and columnar stagger (746759, 332891, 921791). Throughout: do what feels comfortable (425704). **Edennil from 2020 on: rotating is NOT necessary** (461461); you don't type at that angle, you move your arms over for `glhf`/`gg` (485241).
- **If you cannot reach the modifiers without tilting, slide the keyboard left** rather than angling your wrist; the arm stays perpendicular to the board (126155, 258540). One user reports the no-tilt position feels like a claw mouse grip and adds index-tendon and wrist tension (305920) — unresolved.

## Keycaps: pulling, profiles and landmarks

- **You do not need to remove any keycaps** (758484); The Bee's Knees has none removed, Alt being rarely used (885608, 199411), and the full Core+ swap makes removal unnecessary, all three modifiers ending up adjacent (688533).
- **The caps people do pull are the one next to Alt and the one next to `/`, for thumb room** (717544, 386688). Recipes, with photos: pop the key right of the spacebar so your thumb sits in the hole and reaches Shift instead of going over the tops (032293, 950026); pull the caps between right Alt and right Ctrl so the thumb slides between modifiers (472512); pop the caps between right Alt and right Shift, two photo variants circulating, jaydik and Mell00yell00 both on the "bottom" one (994119, 859868), said to ease `/` and Shift (752562), make injects much easier (397760) and stop accidental shift+control (790762); pull the cap next to Control so the thumb reaches Shift cleanly (545418, 936125). bunfoof goes further: he "found it easier to press / with my thumb when I was able to remove both the Fn and Ctrl keycaps instead of just the Fn keycap" (H:284123). **Dissent: rotate the board instead**, going over the top is easy (415637). Siaal moved Alt to the key left of right Ctrl, fixing his opening-sequence cramp (397898); AvroArrow uses the Fn key there (671063). Hoplite's board has Windows and Fn physically removed, leaving a gap next to Shift; v6 works anyway (629308, 490189).
- **The gap a pulled cap leaves is the point, per several users.** jaydik: "I like the gap" and "I actually pop out two" (H:417816); "personally i like having the gap I feel it is the point" (H:874098). Some tape over it instead (H:471845). Mell00yell00 would desolder the switch rather than leave a hole (H:565446) and pulls nothing himself, because his alt next to the spacebar outputs `z`, a trash hotkey, and the key right of `M` is his trash control group (H:825127).
- **Pulling Fn is now optional — a change of position.** Poletes used to proselytise for it, switched boards, forgot, and has had no problems since (998302); Mell00yell00 still pulls it as a tactile reference, not for reach (947627); the middle option is a lower-profile cap (176290).
- **Landmarks by feel are contested.** For: ReinFórce found rubberized caps immediately helpful when panicking and losing finger position (315469, 052998); JohnKavinski rates shorter or taller caps on control groups highly (130688), though taller caps on the *distant* keys later backfired (576040); Soverange's orientation kit is rubber caps in a pattern, **thumb keys flipped 180 degrees so the thumb does not press the key's edge**, a wrist rest, and ~30 degrees of rotation so `4` and `Q` are level (659549, 558078; will3285 followed it, 369843). Against: rubber caps were slower to recognize than a glance (517004) and felt like they slowed Mell00yell00's fingers (382795). **Most people land on tape** — medical or marking tape, ~$3 at Walgreens, enough friction to find home base (524761, 555178, 897542, 609276) — or on removing the cap, trying it yourself being the only real test (162536).
- **Colour works if you use it sparingly.** Sora learned faster with coloured caps (H:073623); Mell00yell00 posted his taihao set for core 6 (H:868810) with the caveat "ive found that you dont want too many or else they dont mean anything" (H:863383).
- **Removing caps to make islands you can find by feel**: right-win and right-alt, `B` and `M` to island `N`, or F6 and F7 to island F5 (126396, 576830, 247869). WASD Keyboards sells multi-colour sets marking the core finger positions (527882).
- **Profile matters more than the board.** The Anne Pro 2 complaint that shift and ctrl are too tall is a keycap profile problem: it ships OEM, DSA caps flatten the difference, and a wrist rest helps as much (390804, 444358, 887690). Cherry profile is the community default (H:382849).
- **Sourcing**: ANSI is easy, "ISO-DE on the other hand..." (H:210398); wasd ships internationally, which is how TobyTheKiwi replaced a broken `E` cap in New Zealand (H:048966, H:760705); also kbdfans and kprepublic (H:109058), thekey.company blank ABS cherry caps (H:134027), taihao rubberized textured caps (H:549407). For background, [keyboard.university](https://keyboard.university) — "The section on keycaps is really useful" (H:195468).

## Switches

- **Weight is the only Core-specific variable: 55-62 g is the sweet spot, over 67 g a struggle.** Mell00yell00 runs 55 g silent tactiles and stays under 63.5 g (954206, 331531); bunfoof's MX Blacks make pinky control groups hard (022656). His numeric rule in the 2021-11 and 2022-02 tutorials: "you want something with a spring weight of 62 grams or less" (H:769034); "once you get into the 67-72 range it starts feeling a little heavier and bettter for typing not gaming" (H:295424); "anything under 55g feels a little too light" (H:436309). **Light springs break your big keys**: under 60 g "will have a tough time returning large modifiers including the spacebar to their starting position" (H:187581), so swap heavier springs under shift and the spacebar (H:125043).
- **The named pick is the [Gazzew Boba U4](https://splitkb.com/products/gazzew-boba-u4-black-silent-tactile-switch)** (H:223552) — silent tactile, about MX red weight; the #general summary gives 55 g and up to 67 g as fine for gaming, shorter travel better, a tactile bump technically suboptimal for speed (155944, 247370), while the tutorials name the 62 g version: "they have a snappy bump and the 62g version is the sweet spot for spring weight" (H:757836), "very quiet and can be used right out of the box... you can lube and film them if u wanna get crazy but not necessary" (H:499605). Edennil runs U4Ts (H:401468). The cheap equivalent is the Outemu Silent Sky, "a smaller bump... equally as quiet" at 62 g (H:134964).
- **Silence is independent of tactility, and 5-pin does not matter.** "a silent switch has dampening pads at the bottom of the switch housing or on the rails of the switch stem" (H:894656), and "there are less silent tactiles out there than silent linears" (H:881440). The extra legs on a 5-pin "can just be clipped off with a set of nail clippers" (H:882698, H:047124). **Dissent on silents**: Poletes, "i hate the idea of silents tbh", they "defeat 1/2 the point" (H:141682, H:708156).
- **Beyond weight, feel is preference** — tactile vs linear has no Core-specific answer (550016) and there was never a consensus recommendation (684618, 720242, for CM Storm Cherry reds and the BlackWidow as a running joke). Edennil's version: browns aren't tactile enough. "cherry browns are linears with a scratch" (H:625610), expanded: "The general trend with all my favorites though, is the tactile bump on the browns is very small. It feels like they had a linear switch with a manufacturing defect that is a scratch" (H:457694), "I strongly prefer having a pronounced tactile bump" (H:157847) — Zealios or Holy Pandas, formerly ergo clears (H:053952) — but "honestly, it's whatever feels good to you" (H:335718). He runs **Zealios v2 at 65 g**, picked with a switch tester, says any of 62/65/67 g has a nice bump, and that the lack of pretravel "doesn't matter at all" for gaming (H:264202, H:306896, H:471839). Mell00yell00's pick is the Kailh Box Noble Yellow then Box White (H:850506); Kailh Box are "basically dustproof because of their design" (H:583539). In use: Edennil's 65 g Zealios in the Redox and Cherry Browns with Green Tea caps in a wood-case Pok3r (638722), marin's Gateron browns (908096), Poletes' Zealios V2, indexing off the gap next to the arrow keys (459647).
- **Buy a switch tester or a hotswap board before committing** — the top line of Mell00yell00's shopping checklist (H:231571), which also covers: PBT caps thud rather than click and are easy to swap; o-rings quieten bottom-out for under $5 at the cost of a squishier landing; most newer boards are programmable without software; Kailh box whites are like MX blues without the rattle but are not quiet; reds and silent reds are near-identical; and "most cherry switches have a 4.0mm travel distance and while others are 3.6-3.1", which o-rings reduce further. Sources: [novelkeys](https://novelkeys.xyz) for switches and testers (H:658724), [zealpc](https://zealpc.net) for Zealios (H:691009).

Edennil: they "100% feel mushy" (H:855914), the point is bottom-out dampening (H:731198), and the "maybe .2 mm... maybe .3" of travel saved (H:486264) "won't make you faster, possibly placebo — The thing is, placebo works" (H:533952). Washers rattle (H:943420). Even "silent" switches are "kind of loud" (H:372855). Anh's o-ring question went unanswered (H:446666).

## Hand position, thumb technique and the spacebar

- **Float your hand; do not rest your wrist on the desk**, or pressing Shift alone gets hard (528534); the hand rests as if holding a ball (995424), specifically a tennis ball (052692).
- **Thumb technique is disputed and morphology-dependent (2020-09).** bro/"vae": rest the thumb tip on the leftmost part of Shift pointing toward `-` and press by *curling* the thumb rather than pushing down from a rigid rest (299667, 380110). **Edennil disagrees flatly** — curling makes it worse — while allowing there is no perfectly right answer (840445, 674837); Poletes says the wrist looks adducted (172133). **Resolution: hand shape** — bro has a wide palm base with short fingers, Poletes a narrow base with long middle fingers and a short thumb, and each finds the other's position painful (043705, 111443). bro's method also needs board rotation (169030).
- **The spacebar is unused by default — claim it and hit it with your palm.** Bind idle worker, escape or the rally camera there (477871, 927857); rest the palm base below the pinky on it, single-press action only (094514, 876560), since a key that large is very hard to double-tap with palm or pinky (890715). Mell00yell00's palm hits space as a matter of course (689188), which works if your forearm is perpendicular to the board (223218). **Edennil is strongly against it**: it drops the hand far more than it should (342022), and that key then can't be a control group or Esc (643809).
- **The pinky gets stronger with use** — kendo, piano and guitar avoid it no more than this does (432118, 113610); Siaal found the `J` pinky control group intimidating and tiring at first, then a non-issue (514049, 363274). **Downward reaches are the real problem, not fingers**: jaydik finds `M`, comma and period harder than `H`, `B`, `G`, `N` (026755, 314854). On strain, the interossei rather than the forearm tendons should drive key presses (469482).
- **Small hands are a real constraint on 6.0**: Poletes could not comfortably reach three of 6g's keys and reverted to a frankencore (212938). For cancel on `7`, pinky is correct but ring is more reliable under pressure (237746).
- **Wrist pain on 6 usually means tilt, chair or desk height, not the layout.** Solarium quit over sore wrists on 2020-06-02 (067348); TOMES blamed a tilted keyboard (563019); streetplay suggested chair and desk height (066596). Unresolved; he left.
- Where people rest: Mell00yell00 on `J K L ;` with equal time on the row above, base cameras on `0 9 8 7 U` (173249, 985696); vorinvictus, larger hands, on `J I O P`, thumb on Ctrl (369600).

- **Ping placement is unsolved.** 6.0's default ping is unreachable; Hoplite floated Ctrl+Space or Ctrl+Space+left-click, and jaydik judged it a reach but too niche to need easy access (226197, 269737, 425468; 230863, 975170, 915034). See [open-questions](/open-questions.md).
- The trash key moves to Backspace (058153) or the Alt right of the spacebar (273281).
- ReinFórce's Terran 6.0 edits: swap `.` and `N`, `N` being hard to reach repeatedly; select-all-army to F8, cycle bases to F9 (702899). Edennil made a similar swap but warns that moving a control group to the middle finger below home row broke other sequences for him (908841). See [layouts-and-variants](/layouts-and-variants.md).

## Mice and mouse buttons

- **Two side buttons are all you need**, and they are the only mouse buttons TheCore binds, so almost any mouse works (262476). Default use is next/previous subgroup, the Core giving you no tab key, and tab reaches the commands of a subset of a mixed control group (196254, 096778, 453439, 110516, 652511, 078500); the pro Core users all have side buttons (525524). The same job in the hardware channel: Rax, Factory and Starport share one control group and you cycle them with the mouse side buttons (H:161404). Budget pick throughout is the **Logitech G203 at about $20** (706134, 080681, 367435, 876511), with the G305 alongside (951828).
- **If your modifiers moved to the mouse, move next/previous subgroup onto the freed keyboard modifiers.** Edennil (H:040892): "If you are using the Core and have moved Control/Shift to the side mouse buttons, I would remap the control and shift buttons on your keyboard to something else and make those be your next/previous subgroup keys", since under the thumb they are "good/easy keys to press" (H:217178).
- **What else goes on them**: select-all-warpgates on Shift + back, leaving the gateway control group as a counter (838566, 846657), or warpgate on ctrl+mouse-back (078879); forward and back to cycle production building types sharing one group (384384); extra camera locations (257128) or extra control groups (040714); cameras and awkward pinky control groups on a G502 (418827). Edennil trades previous-subgroup for Select Warpgates (274506). Warp-in on a side button works because your macro2 group holds only robos and stargates, so next-subgroup is enough (469598), at the cost of one subgroup direction (652892).
- **Do not move game commands onto the mouse beyond tab/back-tab.** SwordSmith proposed attack-move and hold-position there (867321) and was rejected: you push against the mouse and fight your own aim (279794, 047390). The same rule, stated earlier as a hardware fact: eMan found a third top button awkward and side buttons move the mouse, so he couldn't use more than 3 easily (H:446514, H:011530); JaKaTaK wanted three top buttons so middle-click stays available (H:754895); Edennil notes wheel click needs more force (H:358400). jaydik prefers easy finger access to all 10 groups over a many-buttoned mouse (582672, 016806); SieStein agrees (029053); Poletes found zero to four mouse buttons a struggle (087070). Luna Cancels considered an MMO mouse under v5, where control group access was worse, and dropped it after v6 (732504); a Razer Naga was floated to avoid strain (842398). Pokebunny can't use thumb buttons at all, squeezing throwing off his mouse hand (217812) — so tab on `Y` was floated, `U` being a camera location (460608).
- **Keep the middle button free for drag scroll.** Holding the wheel to pan is a camera supplement several people use (971670, 780766) and is better for mid-fight scrolling and most scrolling generally (550485). The wheel is not a subgroup control in SC2 (414674); without side buttons, remap the wheel in mouse software or AHK (545088), use middle mouse and lose drag-scroll, or use Luna's alt+shift+p / alt+shift+j (974528, 001062); nw++ tabs through production with ctrl+P (917184).
- **Weight beats ergonomics, per the one real argument about mouse shape.** Jico says a vertical mouse buys an extra usable finger and finer microaiming (H:234752, H:263454), his Deathadder hurting after a full workday (H:777376). Mell00yell00 counters, 2019-12 (H:929290): vertical mice are too heavy for gaming, "using a mouse under 85 grams that has a good feel is the best way to go", listing xtrfy m4, g-wolves skoll, glorious model D, logitech g403/g703, zowie ec1/2, MM710, g-wolves hati, corsair m65, logitech g102/203. No resolution.
- **Exotica, evaluated and passed over.** The Swiftpoint Z per Jico has buttons at different actuation levels, several per finger, and fingertip buttons (H:341978, H:087168), but is too heavy with slow firmware iteration (H:591242); its up/down finger button is "You curl your finger to pull it up like pulling a trigger, or straighten your finger to press it down", not a motion to do often (H:476354). Cross-device layers: the Dygma Raise hub lets a keyboard key activate a shift layer on the mouse and vice versa, flashed to a low-latency chip (H:710081); Razer Synapse fakes the same thing with a Tartarus layer on a mouse button, "Then mid-game it crashes" / "Synapse is unreliable garbage in my experience" (H:181025, H:106442).
- **For a zero-sensitivity button, use the mouse's DPI toggle rather than a script — and hold it, don't toggle it**, since a toggle costs two clicks (H:311026). VokinLoksar asked for zero-sens and inverse-sens buttons (H:057095); Siaal said AHK does the first easily and the second less so, adding "i just don't know how blizzard feels about you using scripts to control mouse movement" (H:381108), and Mouseinvert and Sakasa were glitchy (H:927644). Mell00yell00's answer (H:260530): set the DPI button to two settings, your normal 400 and the lowest available, and toggle; and "if possible id leave autohotkey out of it" (H:345431). The AHK version, if you insist (H:161478): `z::BlockInput MouseMove` / `z up::BlockInput MouseMoveOff`; it popped a dialog on long holds, so Altafen's fix inserts a KeyWait (H:831588, H:340078): `z::` / `BlockInput MouseMove` / `KeyWait, z` / `Return` / `z up::BlockInput MouseMoveOff`.
- **Axis inversion needs a driver, and that route died.** "the autohotkey inverter is a bit jittery - it waits for the mouse to move and then moves it back the other way" (H:805644), so Altafen wrote `sens_modifiers.cpp` on [Oblitum's Interception driver](https://github.com/oblitum/interception/releases/tag/v1.0.1) (H:193109): `install-interception /install` then reboot (H:175899), run the exe to enable, close the window to disable (H:862879). **Superseded as of 2020-12**: VokinLoksar dropped it "since I also play CS:GO alongside SC2 I had some anti-cheat problems, since they don't allow interception drivers installed" (H:617483); and no mouse ships a hold-to-invert button (H:029023).
- **DPI: lower, with a big pad** (2021-01). jaydik, "Low with a big mousepad would move the onus for aim more to your arm compared to wrist", runs 800 (H:599626); Mell00yell00 runs ~1000 (H:923028); MatosMachine, "I don't think there's really a 'better' dpi" (H:473287) — and whether DPI affects wrist health is left open. The one purchase everyone agreed on was a desk mat, "$20 tops", which also stops the keyboard sliding (H:036820). **Disable in-game sensitivity so DPI is the only variable**: "set a dpi (most like 400-1200) and then disable ingame sensitivity" (H:166282).
- **Mouse software caveats**: G Hub won't rebind scroll and its SC2 auto-profile switching failed; older Logitech software and Corsair iCUE per-game profiles work, but iCUE needs its filepath updated after each SC2 patch (011378).

## Key repeat rate

- **Low repeat delay, high repeat rate is what most people go for** (210567, 774766). Delay is how long you hold before it starts re-sending, rate is how often after that (485254). **Go through the registry to get past the end of the Windows slider** (680444) — "Default windows settings don't have enough range for a lot of people", and the target is "Fastest you can without making mistakes" (H:197140). Tradeoff: too low a delay and you queue up a pile of marines by accident (792470).
- **Numbers**: AvroArrow at 150 ms between repeats gets accidental double-presses; Poletes runs 125 ms, fine with practice (675623). `keyrate.exe` takes delay then rate — Edennil's `keyrate.exe 130 2` is 130 delay, 2 rate, pinned in the Sc2Swarm discord (H:100353) — and he later corrected himself: "I used 130 2, found our the 2nd number was in increments of 10, so I'm actually doing 130 10" (H:546424). Mutaller set delay 200 / rate 2 after seeing [Scarlett at 200/6](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=WPeunkwCgx8) (H:430824). The channel's own pinned message on repeat rate is referenced but never quoted (H:870592).
- **There is a hardware floor at about 16 ms.** Altafen quotes randomascii: "The default timer resolution on Windows is 15.6 ms – a timer interrupt 64 times a second" (H:881082). VokinLoksar measured identical floors for rate 1, 2 and 10 but a real difference at 30, testing at [stereopsis.com/keyrepeat](http://www.stereopsis.com/keyrepeat) and in notepad (H:506688), and ~18 ms inside SC2 (H:270069).
- **Fast repeat alone is a large APM bump**, mostly from rapid-firing lings (369794); J1mm1ny measured about +30 APM (325890).
- **Rapid fire can also be done in firmware, on any key, even where SC2 won't offer it.** Bitstorm's demo output from holding `q` then `w` (H:694814): "hold down `q` then after a bit hold down `w` and `qqqqqqqqqqqqqqwqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqwqw`". Method: "set the key i want repeat to rapid fire mode" (H:248075); "the firmware simulates me pressing the key over and over and again really fast" (H:194956); so "i guess actually with this you can put rapid fire on any key even if SC doesn't support it as a rapid fire option?" (H:032458). Not portable: the board must be supported and writable (H:086676, H:706579), though QMK might widen it (H:898262). AHK can hold-to-repeat instead (H:761281), but Altafen is "vaguely scared of using it in sc2" (H:023744).
- A registry repeat-rate increase made Andrey Alibekov's shift key stick; a Windows 7 problem that went away on Windows 10, stickykeys ruled out (H:175468, H:602881).

## Non-US physical layouts

- **Set your OS keyboard layout to match the file before diagnosing anything.** Laaxus chased a mystery key that was only wrong because Windows was on BE-FR instead of FR (671059, 842749, 826938, 042394).
- **National keyboards need the matching file**, and the project gives them no real support: Swiss QWERTZ (294258), Croatian on a Danish board (375421), a Scandinavian setup (595716). No Colemak or Dvorak file exists (609888, 119360). thenanox on a Spanish board reads the home row as `JKLÑ` (897172); Moss on Scandinavian plans around `ö`, `å` and `+` (641090).
- **The recurring failure is a key that means something else on your physical board.** A German QWERTZ user saw spore crawler as `^`, the German hotkey file being in use when the intended key is `;`, right of `L` on US QWERTY (921508, 328192, 889930). Stefan found all `\` binds dead and believed they should be on `#`, `\` sitting next to left shift there (887922, 193546). AltGr not registering as Alt (see the modifier section) is the third variant.
- **On a German board, the bottom row is partly rescuable in software.** For Solenoid's logilink ID0127 with an AltGr / Fn / context-menu / Strg bottom row, Altafen: "context menu button is rebindable with software", "(altgr is slightly harder, and fn is usually not without drivers)" (H:764511, H:096565).
- **Alternate typing layouts came up repeatedly and nobody switched.** Dvorak, Colemak, Workman and plover/steno were all discussed; Altafen reached 20/30/50 wpm against 150 on qwerty and quit (429046, 621505). The one person who did use Colemak pushed it into firmware rather than the OS (H:447306, H:033280). A Dvorak-plus-AHK chain that behaved as if Ctrl were stuck went unanswered (H:365835).

## Linux

- **Wolta Horo's xmodmap toggle is the working Linux answer for the modifier rotation** (H:418944) — it flips between stock and TheCore mappings without alt-tabbing out of the game:

  ```
  #!/bin/bash
  if [ -f /tmp/sc2toggle ]; then
    xmodmap $HOME/.SC2-off
    rm /tmp/sc2toggle
  else
    xmodmap $HOME/.SC2-on
    touch /tmp/sc2toggle
  fi
  ```

  `.SC2-on` does `clear control / clear shift / clear mod1`, then `keycode 37 = Control_L`, `50 = Shift_L`, `62 = Control_R`, `64 = Alt_L`, `105 = Alt_R`, `135 = Shift_R`, then `add control = Control_L Control_R / add shift = Shift_L Shift_R / add mod1 = Alt_L Alt_R` (H:166110, H:037918); `.SC2-off` restores the defaults (H:763479). In his words, it "will toggle me between TheCore+ and TheCore modes" (H:122112). Setup: Razer Blackwidow Ultimate 2013 on Arch with GNOME (H:664725), SC2 through Lutris "just out of the box" (H:233812, H:229962).
- **Razer macro keys do not work on Linux and cannot be remapped there** (H:124205) — which is why Wolta Horo moved to a QMK board.
- **Buying a full-size QMK board for Linux is hard.** bobo38's constraints (H:352225, H:613141): wired USB, US QWERTY 104 with a big left shift, full size with LEDs and numpad, "programmable without software drivers" — and he could not find a straight full-size QMK board with arrows and numpad (H:601612).
- **The last open thread, 2026-06-04, is Linux plus AZERTY.** Bombyx Mori moved to Linux via Heroic; the queen key `^` now produces `²`, the file calls it `[Grave]` while the game shows `[twosuperior]`, and AZERTY dead-key behaviour looks like the cause; it works if he runs the keyboard as QWERTY (H:165680, H:660779). "Try proton-ge instead" is the last word (H:378169).
